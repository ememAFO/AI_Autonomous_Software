# Human-Gated Pilot Offer

## Pilot proposition

A small, time-bounded test of a quote-outcome decision-support workflow for UK
electrical contractors.

The pilot uses the participant's existing quotation process. It does not require
new public software and does not send customer messages automatically.

## Minimum pilot design

### Setup

- confirm target-market eligibility;
- agree a participant token;
- define the quotation population;
- document existing software and manual process;
- approve data-minimisation rules;
- define stop rules and customer-message approvals.

### Baseline

Use the longer of:

- 14 calendar days; or
- 10 eligible quotations.

Record:

- number issued;
- accepted;
- declined;
- deferred;
- expired;
- unclassified;
- time to decision;
- known loss reasons;
- follow-up attempts;
- estimated staff time.

### Intervention

Run for at least 14 calendar days using a human-approved workflow:

- receipt check where appropriate;
- clarification prompt where appropriate;
- decision or classification check;
- expiry close-out;
- approved stop rules;
- no autonomous customer contact.

### Persistence check

Review again at approximately day 30 to determine whether the process continued
and whether any improvement persisted.

## What the participant receives

- a simple quotation-outcome baseline;
- a prioritised list of process gaps;
- a proposed human-approved follow-up workflow;
- a closing summary of observed outcomes;
- no claim of guaranteed revenue improvement.

## What the participant provides

- anonymised counts;
- bounded outcome categories;
- approximate time spent;
- operational context;
- honest opposing evidence;
- approval before any customer-facing message.

## Exclusions

Do not collect:

- customer names;
- customer contact details;
- precise addresses;
- full quotation documents;
- payment-card information;
- special-category personal data;
- message transcripts;
- login credentials.

## Pilot success is not defined only by more accepted quotes

Useful outcomes include:

- fewer unclassified quotations;
- faster recorded decisions;
- better loss reasons;
- less unnecessary follow-up;
- reduced manual effort;
- identification that the workflow is not worthwhile.

## Commercial-intent boundary

Statements such as “I would probably pay” are not willingness-to-pay evidence.

A qualifying commercial action must be defined in advance, such as:

- purchasing a clearly described low-cost artefact;
- placing a refundable pilot deposit;
- accepting a written paid-pilot offer;
- completing another approved action with real economic consequence.
