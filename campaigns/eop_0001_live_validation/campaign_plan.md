# EOP-0001 Campaign Plan

## Research question

Do UK electricians and small electrical contractors have a meaningful problem
with quotations remaining unclassified, and would a governed decision-support
workflow help them resolve outcomes with less wasted effort?

## Primary hypotheses

### H1 — Outcome visibility

A meaningful subset of the target market cannot reliably state what happened to
a material share of quotations they issued.

### H2 — Prioritisation over generic reminders

Contractors value help deciding which quotations require attention more than a
blanket reminder sequence applied to every quotation.

### H3 — Concrete intent

Some target participants will take a concrete action such as joining a pilot,
sharing anonymised baseline records, or accepting a paid or deposit-backed
offer.

### H4 — Operational effect

For at least some contractors, a governed follow-up and outcome-classification
workflow reduces the proportion of unclassified quotations or reduces time to a
recorded decision.

## Counter-hypotheses

- Most contractors already classify quotation outcomes adequately.
- Follow-up is not a priority compared with lead quality, pricing, scheduling,
  cash flow, or delivery capacity.
- Contractors prefer capabilities already available in their current systems.
- The proposed workflow adds administration rather than reducing it.
- Customers dislike or ignore follow-up.
- Improvement is temporary and disappears after the pilot.
- The problem exists but is too small to justify payment.

## Target participants

In scope:

- UK-based electricians;
- electrical contractors;
- small electrical business owners;
- estimators or administrators who manage electrical quotations;
- firms that issue quotations for domestic or light-commercial work.

Out of scope for primary validation:

- consumers requesting electrical work;
- students without current quoting responsibility;
- software vendors;
- general business owners outside the electrical trade;
- the project owner, collaborators, test accounts, or synthetic respondents.

## Success signals

Signals should be interpreted together rather than used as a single pass/fail
score.

- repeated evidence of unclassified quotation outcomes;
- concrete examples from recent real quotations;
- willingness to share anonymised baseline data;
- willingness to join a pilot;
- observable reduction in unclassified outcomes;
- reduced time to decision;
- reduced unnecessary follow-up;
- sustained use after the guided intervention;
- willingness to pay or make a deposit;
- negative evidence that clearly narrows the target segment.

## Stop or redesign signals

Pause or redesign when:

- public responses are mostly outside the target market;
- participants report no meaningful outcome-visibility problem;
- existing software already solves the need adequately;
- the workflow increases administrative effort;
- the intervention produces no meaningful or persistent effect;
- participants will not share even minimal anonymised baseline data;
- only supportive opinions appear, with no concrete action;
- consent, separation, or provenance controls cannot be operated reliably.

## Campaign phases

### Phase A — Public discovery

Use one approved public post and the provider-neutral form. Public responses are
discovery inputs and remain hold-only under the current evidence taxonomy.

### Phase B — Direct interviews

Select target-market participants with relevant recent experience. Conduct
bounded, consented interviews. Capture only sanitised summaries.

### Phase C — Concrete action

Offer a waitlist, pilot place, or clearly priced low-risk commitment. Record the
action separately from opinions.

### Phase D — Operational pilot

Collect a baseline, run a human-gated workflow, measure effect magnitude and
persistence, and record confounders. Store these records only in the Stage 4G
v0.2 operational-measurement lane.

## Claim boundary

The campaign can establish evidence of a workflow problem, target-segment
interest, concrete intent, and measured operational change.

It cannot establish broad market prevalence, guaranteed revenue impact, or
causal effect across the UK electrical sector without a stronger study design.
